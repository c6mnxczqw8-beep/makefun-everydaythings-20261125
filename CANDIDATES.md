# Everyday-things candidates (draft pass)

Two lines each, at most 400 characters total: line one is an
analogy explaining an everyday thing; line two is the honest
admission the analogy needs.

1. Snooze loan: "A snooze button is a small loan you take
   from your future self, in units of nine minutes." /
   "The interest is paid in panic." -- clean analogy; the
   second line could add that the borrower never consented.
2. Group chat: "A group chat is a meeting that never ends
   and mostly happens without you." / "You are still
   responsible for everything said in it." -- true, the
   analogy is thinner.
3. Fridge light: "The fridge light is a night watchman for
   food you will never eat." / "He has seen things. Mostly
   leftovers." -- charming, more whimsy than explanation.
4. Password: "A password is a secret you keep from everyone,
   including, eventually, yourself." / "The reset email is
   the real password." -- strong second line.
5. Queue: "A queue is a promise that order matters more than
   speed." / "Nobody believes it at the front or the back."
   -- flat second line.

## Verdict

Winner: candidate 1, after a refinement pass. The analogy is
exact (nine-minute units are really how the loan is
denominated), and the shipped second line adds the honest
part the draft only implied: the borrower never consented.
"The interest is paid in panic" stays as the hinge between
the two.
